## Where the rate comes from

Earlier projects recorded **active** hours per point at each phase retro — session time minus
breaks. Bushel is the closest match to Haul: same stack, farm products, weekly cutoff.

| Project | Source | Active h/pt |
|---|---|---|
| Bushel | `bushel/docs/RETROSPECTIVES.md` lines 112–318 | 0.25–0.80, mostly 0.27–0.35 |
| Crewbook | `crewbook/docs/RETROSPECTIVES.md` lines 20–173 | 0.21–0.31 |
| Muster, early phases | `muster/docs/RETROSPECTIVES.md` lines 704–788 | 0.145 → 0.130 → 0.122 |

**Why these are a floor, not exact.** The active-time measure was retired fleet-wide
(`seeds/docs/decisions/DEC-S026-throughput-points-per-calendar-week-replaces.md`). It inferred
breaks from the session transcript, and when the transcript was unreadable it counted no breaks —
so some phases' "active" is really wall-clock. Seeds' conclusion: Claude does most of the typing,
so the developer's hours are not the scarce quantity, and the workflow now tracks points per
calendar week instead. Active hours remain the right input for pricing attention.

## Wall-clock, for comparison

Muster's session files (`started` / `ended` / `points` frontmatter on its `sessions` branch, read
2026-10-25) give a different number, and it is not the basis above:

| Muster sessions | Count | Wall-clock h/pt | Phases 0–2 at that rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| ≤ 12 hours long | 25 | 0.57 | ~79 h |
| All with a duration under 24 hours | 47 of 118 files | 0.97 | ~135 h |

Wall-clock counts every hour a session was open — muster's plan warns it is "inflated by
overnight gaps" (`muster/docs/PROJECT_PLAN.md` lines 20–21), and 22 of the 47 sessions ran over
14 hours. It measures time set aside, not time worked.

## Why the range runs high

These are judgement, not measurement:

- **Haul's Phase 0 builds from nothing** — the domain core, ports and gate that later phases stand
  on. Bushel's and muster's fastest phases came once that existed.
- **Square is new ground.** Tasks 1.2, 1.4, 2.2, 2.3 and 2.13 are 37 points against an API this
  developer has not built on before.
- **The money path** (the charge run, declines, retries) is where a task most often re-points
  upward.
